> For there to be organization, there must be interactions. For there to be interactions, there must be encounters; for there to be encounters, there must be disorder (agitation, turbulence) — Edgar Morin

*Order*

The manager of a company that uses traditional management tries to keep people, processes and resources in balance in order to reach maximum productivity (efficiency) — as if the company were a machine.

*Disorder*

A team in a company that is organized for complexity seeks imbalance in order to foster learning and create innovation (effectiveness) — as if the company were a living being that adapts to its environment to survive and thrive.

*Order*

The traditional management model seeks stability: the "comfort zone" for employees, the "power zone" for managers and planned financial returns for shareholders. To get there, they need rigid processes, career plans, plan-driven management and command-and-control.

*Disorder*

The new management models seek continuous improvement, adaptation to reality and innovation. They also foster learning and see people as essential to the business. As a result, they achieve superior financial returns. To get there, they need the courage to make mistakes, a clear purpose and clear objectives, value-driven management and autonomy-and-collaboration.
